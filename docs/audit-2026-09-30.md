# Audit, 30 September 2026

A full review of the app as it stands after timesheets
([ADR 0022](adr/0022-timesheets.md)): reliability, security, the screens
and the freelancer pages, the flows each kind of person goes through,
bugs, and how the system holds up as the data grows. Done before starting
freelancer invoices, so the next stretch of work fixes what matters most
first.

**How it was done.** Five separate reviews read the code (the server; the
shared sync and views; the office screens in two halves; the freelancer
pages with the flows for each persona), each reporting findings with the
file and line. On top of that the app was run locally with the made-up
data: every screen was screenshotted on a phone (light and dark) and a
laptop with the browser's console watched; the freelancer pages were
rendered; hostile input was pushed through every field that reaches a
page; the device's data model was timed at 11×, 31× and 51× the made-up
data and profiled; the web bundle was measured; the server's headers,
error answers and timings were checked; and the live Railway service's
settings were read. The findings that matter most were then reproduced by
hand (they say "verified"). Line numbers are as of commit a1a8585.

Priority: **P0** fix before real data goes in; **P1** the next week or
two; **P2** worth doing; **P3** polish or later.

**Where it stands.** Round one went in on 1 October 2026: findings 2 to
6 are fixed, and each says how below. Finding 1 waits on Colly's keys.

## The short version

The foundations are sound. Sign-in, the command log, the change feed,
idempotent retries, backups with a test restore, the export, the escaping
on the freelancer pages, SQL parameterisation and both Google flows all
check out. The 302 automated tests (15 shared, 248 server, 39 browser)
pass, the server ones on real Postgres too. Nothing found lets an outsider
in or leaks one person's details to another.

What the audit found is mostly about **finishing the loops the app
starts**:

- things that can be created but not corrected (a person's phone number,
  a crew call's time, a phase's crew when the phase moves);
- answers that arrive but nobody is told about, and changes the office
  makes that never reach the freelancer;
- four ways a phone can be left stuck "offline", broken or blank by one
  bad change, a big backlog, a deploy, or a full disk;
- two small but nasty traps on the freelancer's page (Enter accepts the
  job at the offered rate; the error message lands off-screen);
- a data model that is recomputed in full, and saved in full, on every
  change and every 30-second tick, which will get slow as the years'
  jobs accumulate.

None of it needs a redesign. The fixes are listed in order at the end.

## Findings

### P0: before real data goes in

**1. The live app is open, with no backups and no alerts.** Known and
waiting on keys, but it heads the list: anyone with the address can use
every screen, **Download everything** (everyone's phone numbers, rates
and notes) and **Start fresh**. Backups are off, so a mistake or a
database fault is unrecoverable; error reporting is off, so nobody would
know. Switch on sign-in and the backups bucket before the first real
person or job goes in ([sign-in-setup.md](sign-in-setup.md),
[backups.md](backups.md)).

**2. One bad change can strand a phone for good** (verified). A handler
error the server doesn't expect (anything but a `Refused`) rolls the
whole push back and answers 500 with Postgres's own words. The phone then
shows "No signal", keeps its outbox, and resends the same batch every 30
seconds forever; every later change on that phone waits behind it. A date
like `2026-02-31` gets through the shared schema (a regex, not a real
check) and does exactly this: `date/time field value out of range`. The
same date makes the phone's own `view()` throw (`Invalid time value`), so
that phone is blank until its storage is wiped. Browsers' date fields
won't produce it, but any future handler bug would end the same way.
Fix: in `commands.ts`, turn an unexpected error into a rejection with a
plain reason and report it to Sentry; check dates properly (`isDay`
exists) and timestamps (`datetime()`) in the shared schemas; make
`view()` survive a mutation it can't overlay by moving it to the
problems.

*Fixed, 1 October 2026.* An unexpected error rolls back that one change,
answers "The server couldn't apply this change, so it was dropped. Check
it and send it again.", is logged and reported by the command's name, and
is recorded so a resend gets the same answer; the whole push, and the
rest of the phone's changes, go through. (The calendar sync still gets
the fault, so it tries again next round.) A device's own timestamp that
Postgres wouldn't take falls back to the time it arrived. Every date
goes through one `day` schema that checks for a real date in the years
1900 to 2999, in plain words; scan times are checked as timestamps. A
change the view can't show is turned away on the device before it's
saved, and one saved by an older build is set aside as a problem when
the app opens, so the screens never go blank. A request that fails on
the server answers one plain line, never Postgres's words.

**3. More than 500 changes waiting strands a phone the same way**
(`protocol.ts:22`, `mutations: max(500)`). A phone that does a big day's
scanning offline, or any phone replaying what it sent after a backup
restore (`restart()` replays up to 2,000), gets a 400, shows "No signal"
and never syncs again. Fix: push in slices of 500 and keep each slice's
results (duplicate answers already make that safe); a longer timeout for
pushes than the 10 s pulls get.

*Fixed, 1 October 2026.* The outbox goes up in slices of 500, each
answered and saved before the next, stopping at a stale answer. A push
gets 30 s, a pull 10 s.

**4. A deploy breaks the app that's open** (verified: a missing chunk is
answered with `index.html`, 200). The service worker updates itself,
takes over the open page and deletes the old files; the old page's first
lazy load, the camera's QR reader on an iPhone or laptop, or the error
reporter, then fails until someone reloads by hand. Old code also keeps
running against the new server, and a change it doesn't recognise (a new
entity, as timesheets were today) is dropped while the cursor moves past
it, so that phone misses those rows until it starts its copy afresh.
Fix: reload when a new version is in control (or show "New version, tap
to reload") and on a failed lazy load; answer 404 for a missing
`/assets/` file; version the snapshot so old data isn't read by new code.

*Fixed, 1 October 2026,* apart from the versioned snapshot. A new version
waits until someone taps Reload on a bar at the top ("A new version is
ready."); a tab left open looks for one every hour; a page whose file
has gone reloads itself, once a minute at most and never while offline.
A missing `/assets/` file answers 404. A change for an entity this
build doesn't know is kept under its name for the build that will,
which covers what the snapshot version was for; a version number can
still come if the shape of a known table ever changes.

**5. On the freelancer's page, Enter in the rate field accepts the job**
(verified: a counter of €350 became "accepted", at the offered rate). The
rate input sits in the same form as Accept, which is the form's first
button. Fix: the counter-offer in its own form.

*Fixed, 1 October 2026,* another way: the form's first button is now one
drawn out of sight (not hidden, which Safari before 16.4 would pass
over). The server treats it as Send rate when a rate was typed, and
otherwise asks for a tap. It can never accept.

**6. The freelancer's message lands off-screen** (verified: the notice
sat 128 px above the top of the screen). Every answer redirects to the
offer's card, but the message ("Tick at least one day", "Already booked
on Harbour Lights that day") is drawn at the top of the page. A refused
accept looks like nothing happened. Fix: draw the message inside the card
it's about, and treat it as an alert, not a status.

*Fixed, 1 October 2026.* The message is drawn inside the card the page
lands on, as an alert in the burnt-orange tone when it's a refusal.
Messages with no card (after a decline, say) stay at the top. The
timesheet page does the same, and an explanation ("there's no timesheet
here for you") is a warning, not a refusal.

### P1: the next week or two

**7. People can't be corrected.** Once added, a person's phone, email,
rate, skills and name can't be changed anywhere (`person.upsert` exists;
only the Add form uses it), and the call sheet even sends the office to
"add one on the Crew tab" to do it. A wrong digit breaks WhatsApp, texts
and "who to ring". Freelancers can't fix their own details on their link
either. Nobody can be archived or removed, so leavers stay in every
"Offer to…" list, and there is no right-to-erasure path (the download is
built, the deletion isn't).

**8. Crew calls can't be changed, and a phase that moves strands its
crew.** There is no `call.update`: a call time, rate, details, how many
or dates can only be cancelled (which tells everyone it's withdrawn) and
asked for again. Moving a phase leaves its calls on the old days: the job
page warns, the planner shows amber, but the freelancer's page, feed and
call sheet keep the old days and the calendar re-makes the day with no
guests. Fix: `call.update`, a "Move the crew too" choice when a phase's
dates change, and a prompted message to those booked.

**9. Nobody is told.** The app sends nothing itself, by design, but it
also never *prompts* the office to send anything after the offer: not
after Confirm, Withdraw or Release, a cancelled job, a moved phase, a
changed call time or an approved timesheet. The freelancer finds out only
by opening their link. The other way round, the office isn't told when
answers or timesheets arrive: no count on the Crew tab, and declines
never appear in "Answers to check", so a call left short by a decline has
no queue. Fix: open the Send panel after those actions with a prefilled
message; a count on the Crew tab; declines in the queue.

**10. A booked freelancer has no way back to the office.** Once
confirmed there is no "I can't make it" or "running late" (the
architecture promises both), and none of the three pages carries the
office's phone or email. Fix: an office contact block on every page, and
a "Can't make it" post that lands in Answers to check.

**11. Refusals are shown in the wrong place, or as alerts.** A change the
server turns down appears only on the screen of its own area: an offer
sent from a job's page is refused on the Crew tab; a scan refused on a
pick list is invisible from Stock or the job. The top bar counts only
what's waiting, never what's failed. Nine mutations are sent with no
catch at all (a local validation failure then does nothing, silently),
and 33 places use the browser's own `alert()`/`confirm()`, unstyled and
blocking in a home-screen app, some showing zod's wording ("Number must
be less than or equal to 100"). Fix: a "not done" count in the top bar
with one list; one `act()` that catches and shows the reason inline;
in-app confirms with the consequence in words.

**12. Every 30 seconds the app rebuilds and re-saves everything, even
with nothing new.** A sync tick rebuilds the whole view three times and
writes the whole snapshot to IndexedDB whether or not anything changed;
every server poke rebuilds the view just to read a number; the pick list
rebuilds it five times per scan. The rebuild itself has three hotspots
that between them are most of its cost: a date formatter built per
inspection record, offers scanned once per call (calls × offers), and a
collator built per kit-line comparison. On a busy year's data that is
seconds of main-thread work per tick on a phone. Fix: skip the save and
the rebuild when nothing changed; expose the cursor; cache the last view;
group offers by call; cache the formatter and collator. Together these
should cut the rebuild by two thirds and the idle cost to nothing.

**13. Storage can hang, blank or fight.** The one-record IndexedDB save
has no `onabort`, so a write that fails at commit (quota) never settles,
and from then on every form hangs with no message. The app boots on a
top-level `await` of that store with no fallback: if IndexedDB is
unavailable or corrupt, the page is white. Two office tabs each hold
their own copy and overwrite the same record, last writer wins. Fix:
reject on abort and say "this phone is out of storage"; fall back to
memory with a banner; a single-writer lock or reload the other tabs.

**14. "Booked" means two things.** The job list and summary ("6 of 10
booked") count people who have accepted but aren't confirmed; the call
sheet calls the same people "To confirm". Pick one: booked = confirmed,
and show "5 booked, 1 to confirm".

**15. Money typed in is parsed loosely.** "1,250" becomes €1.25 and is
saved; "€250" becomes NaN and an alert. Fix: one parser that accepts
euro signs, spaces and thousands separators, with an inline message.

### P2: worth doing

**16. Screens are walls on a phone.** With the made-up data the Crew tab
is 10,900 px tall, Stock 11,800 px and a job's page 7,400 px: every crew
call is fully open with its own "Offer to…" select, every form is inline,
and the select lists every person with no search or skill filter. At a
real year's scale the Crew tab is unusable. Group calls by job and date,
collapse filled ones, put forms behind a button, and a person picker
with search.

**17. Batch PAT testing records a pass the instant a label is read**,
with no undo for the wrong item; only "It failed" is offered afterwards.
Hold each read for a few seconds with Undo, or ask for a "Passed" tap.

**18. Scanning gaps.** "Put an item here" and "Put an item in a case"
take a typed number only, with no camera. The camera and search close
after every lookup, so each item needs Scan again. A link opened inside
WhatsApp or Gmail's browser, or over plain http, says "There is no
camera on this device" instead of "open this in Safari or Chrome". On
Android the reader scans the whole frame, on iPhone only the box, so a
neighbouring label can be read early. No zoom for small codes.

**19. Nothing created by mistake can be voided.** A product that ever
had an item or a fault can never be removed (retire reasons are sold,
scrapped, lost, stolen: no "added by mistake"); a label run of up to
10,000 numbers goes in with no confirm and can't be cancelled; a fault's
"what's wrong" can't be edited though the command allows it; a wrongly
typed "where" silently creates a place.

**20. Server hardening.** Hashed assets are served `max-age=0` (the
service worker hides it, but first loads revalidate every file). No
security headers (`X-Content-Type-Options`, `frame-ancestors`, HSTS).
CORS reflects any origin (harmless without credentials, but pointless).
5xx answers carry Postgres's error text. `/api/export` is a GET with a
side effect (a history row). Backups hold every freelancer's link token
in plain text while the export strips it. An unhandled rejection in
`poke()` (a database hiccup right after a push) would exit the process;
the pg pool has no `error` handler; a calendar sync pass that fails is
not retried until the next change. The freelancer page runs one query per
job the person was ever offered.

**21. Freelancer page details.** Reply-by is never set by either office
form though the page shows it. The day picker pre-ticks days the person
is booked elsewhere, so the server's refusal is the first they hear of
the clash. The calendar feed address is inline text with no copy field,
and Google Calendar on a phone can't add it. Staff see "rate to agree".
"Call 08:00" where forms say "Call time". "Earlier" mixes past work with
declined and withdrawn offers, all "Confirmed" or "Withdrawn" with no
date. The `?m=` message is echoed from the address (escaped, text only).

**22. Accessibility.** Input borders are 1.4:1 against the card (WCAG
wants 3:1), so fields dissolve in daylight. No focus moves on navigation
and the page title never changes. No `<main>`. The back link is 28 px
tall. The current tab (3.99:1) and the grey pill (4.1:1) are the two
known gaps. "Not done" isn't a live region. Some labels are placeholders
only (contact fields, the away note).

**23. Offline overlays that drift from the server.** Cancelling a call
offline leaves its offers "Confirmed" until the sync; confirming a
counter doesn't carry the new rate into timesheet totals; sort ties fall
in arrival order, so two devices can show different orders; a phase
added offline loses its Change button; the Ask-for-crew form's phase is
fixed when the page opens.

**24. Design-system drift.** Status pills are re-implemented in six
places, "Waiting to sync" is typed nine times, `useView` four times,
`today` five (two of them UTC and fixed at load, so wrong after
midnight), `act` twice; ten Stock screens wear `app crew jobs warehouse`
to borrow other areas' CSS; six phrasings of "nothing here yet"; a
scatter of off-scale px and eleven ad-hoc font sizes though the design
system defines 13 text styles.

**25. The laptop shows a phone.** Every screen but the planner is a 640
px column in the middle of the screen with the tab bar across the
bottom. That's the design system's rule, and it's consistent, but the
office lives on a laptop: Jobs (list beside job) and Stock (catalogue
beside detail) would earn two columns at 960 px and up.

**26. Installing on a phone.** No `apple-touch-icon`, only an SVG, so
"Add to Home Screen" on an iPhone gets a screenshot as its icon;
`lang="en"` rather than `en-IE`.

**27. Tests.** Nothing tests the sync client's paging, restart after a
restore, "cleared", a stale push or a >500 outbox; the shared views are
tested only through the server's tests; no test covers a deploy while the
app is open; nothing checks that every command has its words in the
history (all 47 do today).

### P3: polish or later

- The History tab is one long wall; grouping by day and person would
  help, and while sign-in is off every entry says "Someone".
- The Phase 0 sync test is still shipped and reachable from Stock, with
  its own header and UTC date; its `product`/`booking`/`scan` commands
  are still live for any device and appear in the export.
- The change feed is never compacted, so a new device's first sync pulls
  every superseded row; fine for years, but a snapshot endpoint will be
  wanted eventually.
- Pokes and the calendar-feed cache are per instance: with more than one
  replica, phones on one wouldn't hear changes made through the other.
- GDPR intentions in the architecture (roles, personal data in tighter
  tables, deletion on request) are not built; every office device holds
  every freelancer's phone, rate, notes and link token.
- Dependencies: no known vulnerabilities; zod 3, Vite 7, Vitest 3 and
  PGlite 0.3 have newer majors, none urgent.

## What checked out

So it needn't be looked at again soon: every `/api` route needs a session
when sign-in is on, and the public ones are only health, config, me and
the sign-in steps; cookies are HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax and Secure on
https; all state-changing API routes are JSON POSTs, so the form parser
can't be turned against them; OAuth `state` and PKCE on both Google
flows; no open redirects; every dynamic column name comes from a
whitelist and every value is a parameter; every value on the freelancer
pages goes through the escaper (hostile names, notes and extras pushed
through all three pages came out inert); link tokens are 144 random
bits, feed codes are derived and can't be turned back, session tokens
are hashed at rest, the Google refresh token is sealed; the advisory
lock serialises every command and the change feed stays gap-free under
concurrent pushes; mutation ids make every retry safe; the backup is
taken at one consistent moment, checked by a test restore, and restored
all or nothing; the calendar sync's loops are bounded and serialised;
the request log masks private links and drops query strings; Sentry
reports are scrubbed and keep no breadcrumbs or user; dates are handled
in UTC and are DST-safe; money is integer cents throughout; the outbox
is saved before any send and only shrinks on an answer; overlapping
`sync()` calls fold into one; the camera stops when the screen is hidden
and its effects clean up; print CSS isolates labels and call sheets;
text contrast meets AA in both themes apart from the two known pairs.

## The order to fix things

**Round one, before real data (P0, about two days).** Sign-in, backups
and alerts on (Colly's keys). Unexpected server errors become rejections
and are reported; real date and timestamp checks; a view that survives a
bad mutation. Pushes in slices. Reload on a new version and a failed lazy
load; 404 for missing assets; a versioned snapshot. The counter-offer in
its own form and the message inside the card. *Done on 1 October, bar
the keys and the snapshot version (see each finding).*

**Round two, the loops (P1, about a week).** Edit person, and let
freelancers correct their own phone and email; archive a person.
`call.update` and "move the crew too". Prompted messages after Confirm,
Withdraw, Release, a cancelled job and an approved timesheet; a count on
the Crew tab; declines in the queue; an office contact block and "Can't
make it" on the freelancer's page. One "not done" indicator and inline
errors instead of alerts. The idle sync cost and the three hotspots;
storage abort, fallback and single writer. "Booked" means confirmed.
The money parser.

**Round three, alongside invoices (P2).** Crew and Stock screens grouped
and collapsed, a person picker with search; batch-test undo; camera in
the put-away flows; "added by mistake"; the server hardening list; the
freelancer page details; accessibility; overlay drift; the design-system
tidy; two columns on a laptop; the home-screen icon; the missing tests.
