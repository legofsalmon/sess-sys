# Audit, 20 October 2026: real size, by role

A full review of the app two days after the 18 October audit. That audit's
P0s were fixed by amending [ADR 0001](adr/0001-sync-engine.md) and, as the
README and [ADR 0067](adr/0067-the-audits-p2-and-p3s.md) record, switching
backups and alerts on, and its P1s by [ADR
0049](adr/0049-holding-up-at-real-size.md). The rest of the week's work was
in too ([ADRs 0049 to 0067](adr/), ending with ADR 0067, which closed the 18
October P2s and P3s). The biggest of it:

- long lists drawn in a window ([ADR
  0050](adr/0050-long-lists-drawn-in-a-window.md)) and a job page in parts;
- Google-only sign-in for staff, a freelancer role in place of the warehouse
  one (the warehouse stays a persona in this audit; on the app, whoever was
  the warehouse now signs in as a head of department, [ADR
  0051](adr/0051-freelancer-in-place-of-warehouse.md)), system admins, and
  narrower reading of other people for crew and freelancers;
- the stock bring-in from Current RMS ([ADR
  0056](adr/0056-bringing-the-stock-in-from-current-rms.md)) with one place
  for a whole list;
- Tidy up for what the calendar brought in, combining people brought in
  twice, first and last names, making another email the main one, a photo on
  a person's page, skills as picks;
- charts on Home, venues on the map, notifications.

Work merged after that commit is not covered: a name checked in Tidy up
([ADR 0068](adr/0068-a-name-checked-in-tidy-up.md)), To sort out ([ADR
0069](adr/0069-to-sort-out-today-and-ahead.md)), finding a product by
typing ([ADR 0070](adr/0070-finding-a-product-by-typing.md)), What's
changed and version numbers ([ADR
0071](adr/0071-whats-changed-and-version-numbers.md)) and the local crew
count on a phase.

The question is the same as last time, asked at the company's real size and
as each kind of person: does it stay right and stay up, is it safe, does it
reach a phone on poor signal, and does each screen do its one job for the
person using it. The next real uses are the stock bring-in and roles going
on for staff, so those are where the findings are weighed.

**How it was done.** Three reviews side by side, all against commit 889c636
and all local: nothing live was read or changed (no Neon, Railway, Google,
Gmail or Resend), nothing was written to Google, and nobody was contacted.
Every finding was checked again independently of the pass that found it: by
re-running it on a server of its own and, in the server and screens reviews,
also by reading it against the code, the decision records and the personas
(the speed review had re-runs only). Only what survived is here; where a
re-run was not possible or not made, the finding says so. Nine findings were
reached by two reviews independently and are written once. The audit
machine (4 cores, 16 GB) was shared between the reviews, so every timing is
an upper bound; where two runs of the same thing differed by more than 30%,
both numbers are given. The commit is 20 October's; the reviews ran in the
days after it and finished on Thursday 22 October, which is why a Thursday
appears in finding 57.

- **The server, the sync engine and the data.** Nine areas (the calendar
  bring-in, crew, the database and running it, money and documents, the
  earlier audits re-checked, security, stock, sync, and tests, code and
  docs) on local servers over PGlite or Postgres 16. The volume seed went
  in through the server's own push route: 16,246 commands in 33 pushes,
  about 800 jobs, 100 people, 300 products, 3,000 items, 1,516 kit lines
  and 200 quotes, leaving 18,834 rows in the change feed; one area used a
  larger seed (900 jobs, 17,020 commands from the seed, 17,286 with the
  made-up data).
- **What stood in for the outside.** A proxy adding 10 or 15 ms each way
  for the distance from Railway to Neon; a fake Google Calendar (2,000
  events, 120 guests, 60 venues) for the bring-in; devices driven from
  Node with the app's own sync code. The one file of live facts the audit
  had (5,952 feed rows, 238 products, 0 items, 0 quotes, 1 user, 2 backup
  runs, ten deploys on 19 October) was read to size costs.
- **What was run on the server.** Memory sampled through backups, exports
  and pushes; the slow queries explained; a backup, a restore and a
  shutdown triggered on purpose rather than on schedule; security checks
  on 14 of the server's routes and the app's live connection (the socket
  a phone keeps open for pushes, not the live service); and every test
  suite.
- **Screens.** Eight passes (director, office, head of Audio, warehouse,
  staff crew, freelancer, accessibility and the design system, and volume on
  a phone), each on its own copy of the app seeded with the made-up company
  plus the same volume seed, driven with Playwright: a phone at 390×844 px
  in dark and light, a desk at 1440×900 px, and the volume pass with the CPU
  slowed four times. Every main screen as each role, the freelancer's link
  pages, offline, keyboard, contrast, targets, print, and a stress copy of
  2,000 jobs and 6,000 items for the numbers that say so.
- **Speed.** A phone emulated at 390×844 px with the CPU slowed four times,
  on slow 3G (500 kbit/s, 400 ms) and fast 3G (1.6 Mbit/s, 150 ms); and
  the server on local Postgres behind a proxy adding 10 ms each way. Pushes,
  pulls, the first copy, tab taps, a CPU profile of the Jobs tap, incoming
  changes, two deploys stood in for locally (a copy of the app with a new
  version), a restart under a live phone, a 15 s lock hold, the calendar
  worker, the export and backup, the bring-in's plan, photo upload, the
  slow-query view and a long session.

Priority: **P0** data loss, a security hole, or the office can't work from
it; **P1** before the next real use or bring-in; **P2** worth doing; **P3**
polish. Screen findings also carry the Nielsen Norman severity (NN 0 to 4)
and, after the colon, the Nielsen Norman heuristic the finding fails, with
its number in their list of ten: "NN 3: 1 Visibility of system status" is
severity 3, failing heuristic 1. Personas: **D** director, **O** office,
**H** head of department, **W** warehouse, **S** staff crew, **F**
freelancer. Line numbers are as of 889c636.

## The short version

Most of what was fixed on 18 October holds at real size. A pull answered
while another device pushes loses nothing. The first copy is served
compressed, 299 KB where it was 2.2 to 3.4 MB, and a deploy that changes no
screen costs a phone 8 KB. Backups are on, with two runs on live, and the
README says alerts are too. The security review found no outside hole. On
the screens, contrast passes but for one link, nothing scrolls sideways on a
phone, and the warehouse's whole walk runs offline. There is no P0 this
time.

Most of what the audit found is in five places:

- **A status word said too soon, and the phone's cost at real size** (P1 1
  and 2; P2 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 22, 47). A new phone says "Up to date" three
  to seven seconds in, while its copy takes 11 to 24 s to arrive, so every
  count is wrong under a word that promises it is complete. At full CPU
  speed the gap is about 1 s against 5 to 6.6 s. The Jobs tab still costs a
  second or two on a phone, and the freelancer's page is still sent
  uncompressed.
- **Home and the lists not yet fitted to the role or the volume** (P1 3, 4,
  5; P2 13, 23, 31 to 36, 40, 41, 43, 48). Tiles are offered to people who
  can't act on them, and open the top of an area rather than the list they
  counted. A 300-line pick list is seventy screens long and Money fifty;
  Crew needed lists six months of calls while Home counts eight. Signed-in
  staff land on the office's screens.
- **Bring-ins and edits that lose or double something** (P1 6, 7, 8; P2 17,
  18, 20, 21, 25 to 28, 42, 44, 45, 50 to 53). A second stock bring-in
  doubles every count, combining two people loses a leave allowance, and a
  freelancer's Accept on a countered offer books the old rate. A calendar
  bring-in holds every phone's push for about 22 s. Most kinds of change
  still apply in the order they arrive, so two people on one record lose an
  edit. A retired item stays under Coming back, and a second missing report
  keeps an item missing after it is found.
- **The server's memory, the live address and the dashboard** (P1 9, 10; P2
  19, 54 to 56). The nightly backup's test restore takes the server to about
  1 GB, Railway's limit, and the memory stays. The office can take the
  director's account with the settings ability alone. The database address
  and the dashboard's start command need Colly's hands. And the server's
  test suite fails every Thursday because the test data asks for leave on a
  weekend, not because of anything in the app (57).
- **What reaches a role it shouldn't, and money's words** (P2 29, 30, 37,
  38, 39). Other people's timesheet notes and document dates reach staff
  crew, and clients' contracts open for every role. "Owed by clients" is two
  thirds work never invoiced.

## Findings

There is no P0. A finding marked reproduced was seen to happen, and all but
a few were seen again on an independent re-run on a server of its own;
where only the first run saw it (109, 110) the finding says so. A finding
read from the code and not seen to happen says it was inferred. Where a
finding re-checks one from 18 October it says which.

### P1: before the next real use or bring-in

**1. A new phone says "Up to date" while its first copy is still arriving.**
D O H W S. NN 3: 1 Visibility of system status; the system's rules: show
what is, not what was sent, and no empty state while loading. On a new
phone, after sign-in or a demo persona switch, Home is wrong for five to
twenty-five seconds under a word that says it is complete, and a phone that
loses signal in that window keeps a partial copy for the day and is told
nothing. Measured as a new phone at 800 jobs with the CPU slowed four times:
on fast 3G the status read "Up to date" at 2.9 s and the last page of the
copy landed at 10.7 s; on slow 3G 7.2 s against 24.4 s; at full speed about
0.7 to 1.1 s against 5 to 6.6 s, over 27 to 38 pull pages. Meanwhile the
director's Home climbed from 4 answers to check to 173 and from €10,301 owed
to €815,204; a head read "1 timesheet, 2 kit lines short"; a staff member
read "No bookings coming up" and a wrong leave balance; the warehouse's Home
asserted "Nothing back late, broken or due a test" with 27 jobs with kit
still out and 25 faults still to arrive. The status is worded from the
connection and the outbox only (`web/src/jobs/common.tsx:71-72`); the
device's sync code marks the round as syncing
(`shared/src/sync/client.ts:576`) and keeps a flag while pages remain
(`client.ts:713-718`), but no screen reads either, and on a phone's Home the
status is clipped to 1 px anyway (`web/src/app.css:113`). Fifteen screens
tell people to "check again once it says Up to date"
(`web/src/jobs/JobScreen.tsx:109`, `web/src/jobs/PickScreen.tsx:79`,
`web/src/crew/PersonScreen.tsx:59` and twelve more), so a job opened from a
link reads as gone. The stock bring-in is when every warehouse phone does
its first pull in patchy signal. Both the server and the screens reviews
reached this, and the speed review measured it. **Fix:** While the first
read is still running the status takes the syncing style it already has and
reads "Reading the company's copy…", shown on a phone's Home too, and past
ten seconds "page 12 of 29" (the head of the feed is known from the first
page). Home's cards, the Crew badge and the counts hold a skeleton and never
draw an empty-state sentence until the read lands. "Up to date" only after
the last page. Offline with the read unfinished, "No signal · copy not
finished". The fifteen "check again" lines read the same flag. *Earlier: 18
October 17.*

**2. The Jobs tab still takes one to two seconds on a phone at real size.**
D O H W S. NN 3: 1 Visibility of system status (a second keeps the flow of
thought). At 800 jobs with the CPU slowed four times a tap on Jobs took 1.3
s cold and 0.8 to 0.9 s warm over two runs; every other tab is under 0.7 s,
and Jobs on a desk is 0.4 s. A CPU profile of one warm tap put 822 ms of its
1,788 ms in one place: the Tidy up nudge
(`web/src/jobs/TidyScreen.tsx:49-63`), which compares every pair of dated
jobs (`shared/src/tidy.ts:72-90`), 326,836 pairs at this size of which about
2,700 are near each other, on every tap and again on every change that lands
while Jobs is open (0.86 s a change). It does the work before checking
whether any job came from a calendar (`TidyScreen.tsx:70-73`), so the seed
paid in full and showed nothing; on live, where the 2,002 calendar rows
beside 903 jobs say most came from calendars, the nudge is wanted but is
thrown away and redone each time. The counts and week rows are also rebuilt
on every render (`web/src/jobs/JobsScreen.tsx:273-281`). People tap twice.
Both the speed and the screens reviews measured it; 18 October finding 11 is
half open (0.8 to 0.9 s warm now against 1.3 s then). **Fix:** Check for a
calendar job before any of the work; keep the Tidy up analysis as a part of
the view, rebuilt only when the jobs change, so it survives leaving Jobs;
and in the pair check stop the inner loop once the next job starts after the
current one's end plus the near window, which removes nearly all of the
pairs. Mark the tapped tab selected at once so the tap is seen to land.
*Earlier: 18 October 11.*

**3. Home's Needs attention tiles are offered to people who can't act on
them.** D H S. NN 3: 5 Error prevention; 2 Match with the real world ([ADR
0034](adr/0034-roles-and-what-each-can-change.md) point 7: the app doesn't
offer what can't apply). With rates off (the default) a head's Home says "18
timesheets to approve" and the page it opens says "To approve (97)" under a
hint that the office approves them; with rates on, two thirds of the primary
Check buttons on that page lead to "The amounts are hidden for your role",
because the button is decided role-wide
(`web/src/crew/Timesheets.tsx:49-52`) where [ADR
0062](adr/0062-what-crew-and-freelancers-read-of-other-people.md) made a
head's rates per department. A signed-in member of staff crew sees "3
answers to check", "5 calls still need crew" and "1 timesheet to approve"
and lands on pages with nothing to tap. A director who approves no leave
taps "8 leave requests waiting" and gets "Who are you?" with a picker of
staff names (`web/src/crew/Leave.tsx:150`). All fourteen tiles are built
with no role check (`web/src/home/standing.ts:109-124`), where the Crew
badge was gated after 18 October finding 9
(`web/src/crew/CrewBadge.tsx:26-27`). A tile that is wrong every day teaches
heads to stop reading Home, and staff take the counts as their to-do list.
Both the server and the screens reviews reached this. **Fix:** Give the tile
builder the device's abilities, as the Crew badge has: no crew tiles without
the crew ability, no stock tiles without stock, no kit-short tile without
kit; the leave tile only to someone who can approve leave; and on the
Timesheets page decide Check per row from the record the server already
marks as amounts hidden (`shared/src/reading.ts:141-144`), with "Waiting on
the office (97)" for a head whose rates are off. *Earlier: 18 October 9.*

**4. Home's tiles open the top of an area, or the whole company's list, not
the list they counted.** D O H W S. NN 3: 7 Flexibility and efficiency; 8
Minimalist design; 4 Consistency (three counts of one thing on one screen).
A head of Audio taps "24 answers to check" and gets "Answers to check (174)"
for every department; "18 timesheets" opens 97; "7 faults" opens 24 with 89
buttons; "3 leave requests waiting on you" opens the Leave page at the top,
with To approve 2,049 px down past their own forms. The office's "70 kit
lines short" opens Stock at the catalogue search with the Kit short card
4,229 px down a 4,951 px page; "27 jobs with kit still out" lands 1,201 px
up; "8 calls still need crew" lands on Answers, with Crew needed 1,024 px
down and carrying no count. For a head of Audio the Kit short card is 4,190
px down, and for the warehouse the Going out and Still out tiles land 1,152
px above the card they count. The tile addresses carry no department and
name an area rather than a part (`web/src/home/standing.ts:110-123`); only
Crew › Calls was narrowed by [ADR
0046](adr/0046-departments-on-calls-and-what-each-role-reads.md). The same
is why Home, the badge and the strip disagree everywhere the head looks (24
/ 184 / 174). `personas.md` (line 123) promises counts "each opening the
list that sorts it", and its table of what's left marks "No overview of the
state of things" fixed by Home. **Fix:** Carry the department in the tile's
address and have each list start narrowed to it, with the existing "All
departments" to widen. Give the parts addresses the screen scrolls to on
arrival (`#stock/short`, `#stock/pick`, `#crew/needed`) as Testing and
Repairs already have, and point the tiles at them. The leave tile at To
approve, with Allowances folded. Count the badge and strip from the same
narrowed list. Do the Crew lists first: they are what a head taps daily.

**5. A pick list of 300 lines is fifty-six thousand pixels on a phone.** H
W. NN 3: 8 Aesthetic and minimalist design; 7 Flexibility and efficiency;
Time Scarcity. A confirmed job given 300 kit lines (1,192 items) is 56,142
px and 8,373 words on a phone, 46,128 px even on a desk, because
`web/src/jobs/PickScreen.tsx:322-330` writes out every in-stock item of each
product by place rather than the ones needed: a sub needed 0 of 5 lists 39
numbers across five places, 3,184 numbers for a 1,192-item need, and those
"where to find them" lines are 59% of the page. The scan box is static at
the top, so it scrolls away after the first screen. [ADR
0017](adr/0017-pick-lists-and-scanning-out-and-in.md) chose "each place with
the numbers of the items there" when that was six numbers; a 40-line job is
still several screens of numbers between the scan box and the next row. This
is what the first load-out after the stock bring-in will look like. **Fix:**
One line per product with the place and count ("Bay C1: 6 here"), the
numbers folded under it with the count in the fold's label; rows still to
pick first and the done ones under "Show N done"; the scan field kept under
the top bar the way the week headings are.

**6. Accept on an offer the freelancer has countered books them at the

**Learnings for the design system.**

- A count that is offered is a promise to act: a tile, badge or chip should
  be built from the person's abilities, not only worded for them. The rule
  that "a button that can't work isn't offered" needs to say it covers
  counts and prompts too (findings 3, 34).
- A count's tap lands on the list it counted, narrowed the same way, and the
  tile, badge and strip count the same list; the system should ask for an
  address per part of a screen, not per screen (finding 4).
- A status word is a promise about completeness. "Up to date" must not show
  while a first read is in progress; the system's list of states needs a
  "reading, N of M" state between "loading" and "up to date", with the
  empty states held back until it has passed (finding 1).
- A sideways scroller needs a cue every time, not only on tables: the shade
  that closed 18 October 23 on Money's tables belongs to strips, tab rows
  and any row that overflows (finding 13).
- Fold controls on a long page are targets too: the 44 px rule reached
  buttons and chips but not the folds themselves, chart columns or inline
  links (finding 58).
- Green is for working, and the rule has to name success messages, settled
  states and "enough" explicitly, or they drift back (finding 59).
- An identifier with a hyphen never wraps; the system should give hyphenated
  references a no-break role (finding 41).
- Two actions with opposite meaning do not touch: a minimum gap, or a
  confirm, between an everyday action and a destructive one on a dense row
  (finding 40).
- Dates on a page that spans more than a year carry the year; the date
  format role needs a "with year" variant (finding 89).
- Brand red as "you" (sess-sys's decision) still has to be the one thing on
  a quiet page that is about you; a red button on an empty page reads as an
  alarm (finding 92).
- A link style is a role, not a class: anchors that carry no class fall back
  to the browser's colour; the system's stylesheet should style every anchor
  under the app root unless opted out (finding 62).
- ADR 0067 closed several P3s "across the app" and each was applied only to
  the places named (58, 59, 62, 88). A rule should carry a short list of
  every place it touches, so a later pass can check them.
