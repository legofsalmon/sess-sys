# Audit, 16 October 2026: the real data

A review of the app the night the real calendars came in. On 15 October
Colly switched on Google sign-in, connected the company account and
brought in two organisers' calendars ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-calendar-import.md)):
141 jobs, 149 phases, 66 crew calls, 179 offers, 36 people and 12 venues,
all from 1 October 2026 on. This is the first time the app has held
anything but made-up data, so the question is different from
[the September audit](audit-2026-09-30.md): not "does it hold up" but
"does what came in look like the company, and what will the office see".

**How it was done.** Read-only throughout: nothing in the live database
was changed, nothing was written to Google, and nobody was contacted. The
live database was read with queries (every job, phase, call, offer, person
and venue, the 380 event-days the import remembered, the history, the
calendar link, the users). The 585-row change feed was copied into a local
copy of the app and every main screen rendered with it, on a phone (390px)
and a laptop (1280px), with the browser's console watched. The import code
was read against what it produced, and the Railway logs for the import
checked. Findings say what was seen; line numbers are as of commit 580e1c3.

Priority: **P0** before the office works from it; **P1** before the next
bring-in or a re-import; **P2** worth doing; **P3** polish.

## The short version

The import itself ran clean: 168 and 49 events read, 141 jobs saved in
two goes, no errors, every event-day remembered so a second look brings in
only what is new, the three "Nissan" titles folded into one job with its
Rehearsal, Show and Load out, "Mansion House - Install" from the second
calendar joined the "Mansion house" from the first, job sheets came
through as clean text, and nothing has been written back to Google. The
screens render it all with no errors, and the whole feed is a fraction of
what the September audit timed.

What the audit found is that **a calendar is not a job list, and the app
read it as one**:

- the organisers' calendars hold gigs, but also dry hires, collections
  and returns, site visits and meetings; every all-day event became a
  confirmed job with a Show and a crew call, so Home says eleven things
  are on today and about four of them are gigs;
- Google's invites were read as the app's offers, so the office now has 54
  offers "waiting" that nobody made (32 of them in the past), 41
  timesheets "not in yet" that nobody is expecting, 8 calls that "still
  need crew" from invites the staff never bothered to accept, and 3
  "answers to check" that are two meetings and a gig somebody declined
  in Google;
- 21 of the 28 freelancers are shown by their email handle
  ("Craicaudio", "Scflighting"), the company's own info@ mailbox came in
  as a member of staff, a supplier came in as a freelancer, and nobody has
  a phone number, a rate or a skill;
- the second organiser's tidy titles ("WL - Malahide Build 4/9 & Client
  Walk Through") beat the title parser, so nine events that are phases of
  four jobs became nine jobs of their own, and the same gig on both
  calendars under two spellings is in twice;
- backups and error alerts are still off, now with real people in.

None of it is lost or wrong beyond repair: every job can be renamed,
re-phased or merged by hand, and the import can be run again. But the
office should not work from Home, Crew or the planner until the ghosts
are gone, and the next bring-in (there are more calendars, and January
onwards) should go through a better import first. The order is at the end.

## Findings

### P0: before the office works from it

**1. Backups and error alerts are still off, now with real people in.**
`/api/health` on the live server says `"backups":"off","errors":"off"`.
Finding 1 of the September audit, still open, but the data it protects is
now 36 real people's names and addresses and a year of the company's
bookings. The nightly backup needs the five `BACKUP_S3_` variables
([backups.md](backups.md)); error alerts need `SENTRY_DSN`
([monitoring.md](monitoring.md)). Half an hour of Colly's time, and
nothing else on this list matters as much.

**2. Google's invites came in as the app's offers, and the app is now
asking the office to chase them.** The import treats a guest who never
answered an invite as someone with an offer waiting
(`server/src/calendar/import.ts:121`, `offerFor`), and a past booking as
one owed a timesheet. With a real calendar that gives:

- 54 offers "waiting on the freelancer", 32 of them for days already gone.
  Crew shows them as "(offered)" with an **Offer…** button; a freelancer's
  private link, if it were ever sent, would show "Nissan, 5 October:
  please answer". Nobody made these offers. Most are staff who don't accept
  invites (one member of staff has 19 of the 179 offers) and freelancers
  who turned up without clicking Yes.
- Home says **8 calls still need crew** and the planner "crew still needed
  on 11 days", counting those unanswered invites as gaps.
- Crew's **Timesheets: not in yet (41)**, one per freelancer booking since
  1 October, each with **Ask** and **Fill in**. Ask opens a message to the
  freelancer. Colly's rule is that nobody is contacted, and in any case
  nobody agreed to timesheets for jobs booked by calendar.
- **Answers to check (3)**: a decline on Heineken Greenlight Galway, and
  two declines of "Ryder Cup Meeting" and "Site visit Ryder cup", which
  are meetings, not calls short of crew.

Fix, in the import: a day that has already passed is history, not an
offer. A guest with no answer on a past day comes in as confirmed (they
were on the invite and the day happened) or is left off; a call whose last
day is past is closed, not open. A booking that came from a calendar
doesn't owe a timesheet until the office asks for one (a flag on the
offer, `respondedVia: 'calendar'` is already there to key on). For the data
already in: a one-off tidy that closes the 32 past calls and marks their
offers, which is a server command, not a hand job across 66 calls. *This
is the one finding that makes the screens unusable today, and it is a
day's work.*

**3. The app must not contact anyone, and the screens are full of buttons
that would.** The app itself sends nothing (checked again: no mail, no
texts, calendar invites off, and the other thread is switching the
calendar writer off too). But every "Offer…", "Ask", "Send", "Running
late…" and the Send panel compose a WhatsApp or email to a real person
now, and the import has handed the office 28 freelancers with live
private links. Until Colly says otherwise, the office should know that
the freelancer-facing buttons are armed. Suggest: a company-wide **quiet**
switch on Admin ("Don't prompt messages to crew yet") that hides the
Send panel and the Ask/Offer prompts, on by default after an import, so
the office can tidy the data without a mis-tap sending a WhatsApp to a
freelancer about a job in October.

### P1: before the next bring-in

**4. Every all-day event is a gig, so dry hires, collections and meetings
are jobs with a Show and a crew call.** The titles say what they are:
"Murt lx hire", "Davin lx hire" (14 days), "Show scope drape rental",
"Bold Frontiers Dry hire", "Forfey LA hire", "cms uplighter shop hire"
are kit going out with no crew; "Collect Screwfix", "Return Gorilla",
"Collect Podium Alan Smith", "Return PSI Truss Adapters" and six more
are a van run; "Ryder Cup Meeting", "Site visit Ryder cup", "Dan daly fit
out in Back to basics meeting 3.45" are meetings; "St. Patrick's Cathedral
Install", "Back 2 Basics Gym install", "Guinness lx oad out" are a phase
not a job. Around a fifth of the 141 jobs are not gigs. Each has a Show
phase, a crew call needing as many people as were invited, holds kit a
day either side, counts in "Today (11)" and "The week ahead (14)", and on
Home carries "Crew 1 of 1" as if a drape rental had a crew.
(`import-plan.ts:232`, `parseTitle`, falls back to Show for any title with
no phase word.)

Fix: a **kind** on the job, guessed on the import screen and shown as a
chip to correct before anything is saved: *gig* (default), *dry hire*
("hire", "rental", "dry hire", "package"), *errand* ("collect", "return",
"drop", "pick up"), *meeting* ("meeting", "site visit", "recce", "walk
through"). A dry hire gets a Hire phase and no crew call, and belongs to
the warehouse's day, not "Today" on Home; an errand is a day with a note;
a meeting is a day with people and no kit. Also: "install", "load out",
"rehearsals" at the end of a title with no " - " should split off
(`PHASE_WORDS`, the `strict` flag keeps "Install" from being recognised
there), and "Site visit", "Recce" at the start should too.

**5. The tidier calendar's titles made nine jobs out of phases.** The
second organiser writes "WL - Malahide Build 4/9 & Client Walk Through",
"WL - Fota Build 7/7 & Final Changes", "Mansion House - Launch Event",
"Mansion House - Load Out PA", "Mansion House - Load Out LX", "Coca Cola
Truck - Live RDS", "Coca Cola Truck - Live Limerick". The parser takes
the part after the last " - " as the phase only if *every* word in it is
a phase word (`phasesOf`, `import-plan.ts:192`), so "Build 4/9 & Client
Walk Through" is a job name, and "WL - Malahide" exists three times (the
Build 12 to 14 October, then two one-day "jobs" on the 15th and 16th),
"WL - Fota" three times, "Mansion House" four times, "Coca Cola Truck"
three times, "Fuel Cupra" twice. On the planner the Malahide build is
three rows; on a freelancer's link it is three bookings.

Fix: when the part after " - " *starts* with a phase word, take the
phase and keep the rest as the phase's note ("Client walk through",
"Final changes", "PA"); treat "Live", "Launch", "Event", "Gig" at the
start as Show. And on the import screen, where two jobs found share
their first word(s) and overlap or touch in days, show them together with
"same job?" ticked, so the office merges them before saving rather than
renaming nine rows. The same would catch the second thing: the same gig
on both calendars under two spellings ("Jeremy Niminio" and "Nimino",
26 to 27 November; "Fuel Cupra" and "Fuel - Cupra Floor Package", 25
October; "Mallahide halloween show" from A beside "WL - Malahide" from B).
Shared days plus shared guests is a strong enough match to suggest it.

**6. A title naming two phases on one day books the crew twice.** "Nissan
- Show Day 2/ Load Out" on 7 October is read as Show *and* Load out, so
the six people on the invite are on the Show call (6 to 7 October) and
again on a Load out call (7 October). The job says "9 of 18 booked" for
six people, and the planner lists each of the six as a clash with
themselves ("Booked on Nissan and Nissan"). Fix: when two phases of one
job fall on one day with the same crew, make one call for the pair or
carry the crew on the first phase only; and the planner should never
count one job twice for one person.

**7. People came in as email handles, and two of them aren't people.**
Google had no display name for most of the guests, so the import named
them from the address (`nameFromEmail`, `import-plan.ts:328`): "Craicaudio",
"Scflighting", "Ciandoeslights", "Johnlightmanenterprises",
"Michaelmccormacklx", "Danielwheatleywork", 21 of the 28 freelancers.
One guest with a name has it surname-first. The company's own **info@**
mailbox is now a member of staff called "Info" with a booking, and a
transport supplier is a freelancer. Nobody has a phone, a rate or a
skill, and all 36 notes say "Added from the guests on Google Calendar".
On a call sheet, in the planner's lanes and in "who to ring" these
handles are what the office will read.

Fix: the import screen's people list needs the **name as it will show**,
editable, with the handle-named ones flagged ("needs a name"); role
mailboxes at the company's own domain (info@, accounts@, office@, hello@)
are never crew; and Crew should carry a quiet "7 people need a proper
name or a number" line until they're fixed. For the 36 already in:
editing each person is already built (September finding 7), so this is
the office's tidy-up, helped by a filter on Crew for "named from their
email".

**8. No clients, and a venue on 12 of 141.** Every job card on Home and
Jobs says "No client or venue yet". The organisers put the client, the
contact and the place in the title ("Joanna verve KPMG", "Jazzy 3 arena
dj booth", "Boney M national stadium", "Linda Swp group RHA gallery"):
"Verve" is in nine titles and is plainly a client; "3 arena", "national
stadium", "Button factory", "CCD", "Bord gais", "RHA" are venues. Only
the second calendar sets Google's location field, which is where the 12
venues came from. Fix: on the import screen, match words in the title
against the venues and clients the app already holds (and a short list
the office can grow), and offer the match as a chip; a job with "verve"
in its name gets Verve as its client once Verve exists. And on the
cards, "No client or venue yet" on every one of 141 rows is noise where
the September design rule says healthy is quiet: show it only once the
company has clients, or only on jobs in the next fortnight.

**9. "Show, Show, Show": repeat gigs are phases of one job.** Events more
than 14 days apart are separate jobs; nearer ones with the same name join
(`GAP_DAYS`, `import-plan.ts:130`). So "Block" (a venue) on 16, 23 to 25
and 30 to 31 October is one job with three Show phases, shown on Home as
"Fri 16 Oct to Sat 31 Oct · Show, Show, Show", and "Fran Archetype
Guinness Harp aviva" on three Fridays is one job. For a weekly residency
that's right; the card should say "3 Show days" or the dates. For a bar
that books the company on and off, a job per date might be truer. Either
way the card wording is wrong.

**10. Nothing before 1 October came in.** Whether Colly chose that date
or the calendars start there isn't recorded (the history keeps the
counts, not the "from" date). If the office wants last season's bookings
for the freelancer pages and the history, run the import again from an
earlier date: events brought in before are skipped, so it's safe. Finding
2 has to land first, or every old booking becomes a timesheet to chase.
Suggest the history record the "from" date and the "left out" counts the
screen showed, which are gone once Bring in is tapped.

### P2: worth doing

**11. Long single "Show" phases.** "Mansion house" is one Show of 31 days
(6 December to 5 January) with a two-day Build inside it and three
further "jobs" that are its install and load outs (finding 5). "Mallahide
santa" is a Show of 29 days, the three Ryder Cup jobs 12 days each. These
are hire periods or residencies, and a Show of a month puts the job in
"Today" for a month and holds its kit for 33 days. The Hire kind from
finding 4 covers most of it; the rest wants a word on the import screen
("a 31-day Show: is it one show, or a hire?").

**12. Staff on several jobs a day read as clashes.** The planner's week
shows 10 clashes, five of them one member of staff who is on the invite
for four hires and a gig at once; across the data there are 35 person-days
like it, most of them staff. For staff that's a normal day (and Google
invites to staff are often "so you know"); for a freelancer it's real.
Suggest: a clash is amber for a freelancer and quiet for staff, or only
counted when both calls have call times that overlap; and "Check: offered
X while booked on Y" the same.

**13. The Crew screen is the whole company on one page.** On a laptop it
is 8,400 pixels tall: "Crew needed" lists every one of 66 calls, filled
ones too ("Filled"), each with "Running late…" for calls today, then 36
people. A filled call isn't needed. Suggest: "Crew needed" shows only
calls short of people, with "Show filled calls" folded; the Home tile
already does the right count once finding 2 is in.

**14. Home's "Today" is a list, not a glance.** Eleven rows today, 14 for
the week, each four lines. With real volume (ten things a day in October)
the Director's one job, "are we all right", is a scroll. Suggest one line
per job on Home ("Nissan · Show · 4 of 6 crew · Heritage"), with the kind
chip from finding 4 so hires and errands sit apart from gigs, and the
"Needs attention" tiles above the list on a phone (today they're below
the eleven rows).

**15. The phase and job names are as typed.** Lower-case starts ("bekah
archetype store house", "cms uplighter shop hire"), a trailing full stop
("Singular Bressie Mullingar."), typos ("Mallahide", "Barrack Obama",
"l'acosutic", "upliugher", "oad out"). The import trims spaces and keeps
the rest, which is right (the office knows these names), but a first
letter capital on save would cost nothing and read better on a call
sheet.

**16. The call's "needed" is the invite list, declines excluded.** A call
"needs" as many as were invited and not declined, so a gig where the
organiser invited eight and three came is "5 short" for ever. Once
finding 2 closes past calls this only matters for future ones; there,
the import screen could show "invited 8, accepted 5" and let the office
set the number.

**17. Venue names from Google's location field** come through as the map

**18. The job page for an errand** ("Collect S5000 from murt") offers Kit,

**19. "0/2 days filled"** on a call where four of six are booked on both

## What checked out

So it needn't be looked at again soon: both imports ran without an error
and the counts in the history match the tables; every event-day is
remembered (380 rows) with its calendar, event and title, so looking
again finds only what is new and a second organiser's copy of the same
event is skipped; titles with " - Phase" parsed correctly where the phase
word stood alone (Nissan's Rehearsals, Show Day 1, Show Day 2/Load Out;
WL - Malahide Build 1/9 to 3/9; Coca Cola Truck - Install; Mansion House
- Install); the cross-calendar join by name worked (the Mansion House
install from B joined A's job); job sheets are plain text, none cut
short, no HTML left; venues are one row per place across jobs; guests at
the company's domain came in as staff and the rest as freelancers, with
the account's own address left out; `calendar_days` is empty and the logs
show no calendar write, so nothing has gone back to Google; crew invites
are off; sign-in is on and the one user is the right one; every main
screen renders the real data on a phone and a laptop with no console
errors; and the feed of 585 changes is nothing to the device's model.

**Learnings for the design system.** A list built from someone else's
data needs a *kind* before a *state*: the calendar's events were all one
kind to the app and five kinds to the people who wrote them. And "healthy
is quiet" fails when every row carries the same empty prompt ("No client
or venue yet" × 141); an empty field is a prompt once, not a label.
